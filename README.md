# Coherence as Strong Equationhood

**Side-Failures, Facing Conditions, and the Equationhood License under Structural Flow**

## What is this?

This artifact defines a Structural Flow method for deciding when a candidate relation has earned the status of strong equationhood.

It asks when an equation is more than symbol adjacency, convention, analogy, compatibility, accounting shorthand, or useful notation.

## Why should I care?

Cross-domain work can easily mistake resemblance for structure. A known equation may look like it fits a new framework before the relation has actually been earned.

This artifact slows that down. It gives a test for whether the sides of a candidate relation have distinct burdens, distinct failures, relation-level co-dependency, and a shared invariant that neither side carries alone.

## What is the claim?

The central discovery is that, under Structural Flow, Coherence is the hinge that licenses strong equationhood.

A candidate relation qualifies as strong equationhood only when earned, structurally distinct side-burdens can be held as one jointly holdable relation, and when the relation itself has a failure distinct from either side’s failure.

## What is this not?

This is not a replacement for ordinary mathematics.

It does not claim that definitions, identities, conventions, unit conversions, thresholds, or accounting relations are invalid. It does not certify empirical truth, derive measured constants, or replace formal proof, experiment, observation, or domain expertise.

It is a structural-warrant method for sorting relation types honestly.

## How was this done?

The artifact applies Structural Flow’s hinge grammar, especially Coherence, to the problem of equation translation. It distinguishes side-burden, side-failure, relation-failure, relation-level co-dependency, shared structural invariant, relation packages, side-completion terms, threshold relations, Recognition-stable equivalence, and strong equationhood.

## Where do I go next?

This artifact is especially relevant for later Structural Flow work involving physics translation, equation-shape analysis, GR-facing role translation, and cross-domain relation testing.
